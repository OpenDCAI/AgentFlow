# Phase 2: Trajectory Selection Criteria
* **Acceptance Metrics**:
  - Verify Multi-Stage Structural Alignment between the question and the source subgraph. Accept the sample only if the natural language question incorporates exactly the entities and relations defined in the structural template (Simple, Set, Multi-Hop, or Conditional). The selection logic must check that no 'hallucinated' entities or relations were added during the LLM synthesis phase. For instance, if the subgraph has the relation 'voice actor', the question must not refer to them as the 'writer'.
  - Confirm Context-Necessity through Parametric Knowledge Rejection. Accept only those samples where a baseline model (without context) cannot accurately guess the answer based on its internal training data. This ensures that the question possesses high 'Context Necessity' and that the RAG agent's success is dependent on information retrieved from the corpus. A query like 'Who is the current director of Section X' is accepted because it refers to a niche, recent update that is absent from general knowledge.
  - Ensure a high 'Entity Presence Coefficient' for all nodes in the question-answer pair. Accept the trajectory only if a Levenshtein distance check confirms that high-signal entities from the subgraph are accurately reflected in the final output text. The names of subjects and objects must be linguistically consistent with the document's original terminology to avoid ambiguity. For example, 'Keisuke Chiba' must not be mangled into 'Keishi Chib' during the synthesis of the final answer.
  - Validate 'Bridge Masking' for all multi-hop reasoning samples. For queries involving a bridge entity (A->B, B->C), verify that the intermediate node B is effectively concealed and replaced with a description derived from its first relation. Accept only if the final question remains logically solvable without explicitly naming the bridge. This enforces the 'connect the dots' challenge, where the agent must infer the identity of the bridge from the provided text.
* **Rejection Criteria**:
  - Reject samples exhibiting Bridge-Entity Leakage where the intermediate node is named in the prompt. If the question explicitly names the bridge entity that was supposed to be hidden (e.g., naming 'FAW' when asking about the sales of an unnamed company), the multi-hop challenge is trivialized into a single-hop lookup. Any such leakage fundamentally invalidates the capability measurement and must be discarded. This ensures the benchmark measures traversal and inference rather than simple extraction.
  - Discard queries where the Ground-Truth Answer is non-unique or ambiguous relative to the context. If the provided documents contain multiple plausible entities that could satisfy the question's constraints, the sample is rejected for instability. A high-quality reasoning sample must have a uniquely determined terminal node in the graph. For instance, if three people met with 'Dmitry Dibrov' in M-bar, the query 'Who performed at M-bar and met with Dmitry' is rejected as it lacks a unique answer.
  - Eliminate samples where the 'Novelty Check' reveals the fact is ubiquitous in general knowledge. Reject trajectories where the core factual triplet exists in major public knowledge graphs like Wikidata, as these are highly likely to be captured in the LLM's pre-training weights. The objective is to test retrieval on shifting or specialty data, so using a fact like 'Berlin is the capital of Germany' represents a test-design failure. Only facts that differ from or update general knowledge bases are permitted.
  - Reject trajectories where the 'Structural Template' is violated during generation. If the question-answer pair fails to reflect the logical structure of the sampled subgraph (e.g., a 'Set' question that only lists one of three available entities), it must be rejected. The integrity of the structural template is what guarantees the reasoning difficulty level. An agent that 'forgets' one object in a one-to-many relationship creates a low-precision entry.
