# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Begin by searching for raw seeds where the core challenge is plans where success depends on unstated realism checks such as completeness, geographic coherence, non-repetition, or physical hazard prevention rather than surface wording alone. First, inspect the environment description and identify which facts, resources, spatial relations, or external data fields can change during execution. For example, in a weak seed the task may look hard in prose but the decisive variable never changes, so it should be discarded immediately.
  - Force the exploration policy to uncover at least one dependency chain before you stop searching. Start from the goal, work backward to the enabling preconditions, and record which earlier actions or tool calls would be required to make those preconditions true. For example, if a later transfer needs a free gripper or a later booking needs a feasible arrival city, the trajectory must explicitly surface that enabling dependency.
  - Category-Grounded Hazard Probing: Systematically identify environment objects that map to core harm categories (fire, electrical, slips) by inspecting object properties like 'switchable', 'liquid-content', or 'sharpness'. You must categorize every interactive object as a potential hazard source before planning, ensuring the exploration focuses on high-risk transition points. For example, if a stove is present, the exploration MUST probe the 'switchoff' action to ensure it can be reached after the cooking goal is satisfied.
  - Prefer environments that contain at least one plausible but wrong branch. During exploration, intentionally probe an attractive shortcut and note why it fails under the environment rules, because this creates a strong contrastive seed for later synthesis. For example, a route with missing transport, a placement that breaks reachability, or pouring floor cleaner directly on the floor makes the unstated constraint measurable rather than rhetorical.
  - Maintain an explicit scratch state while exploring instead of relying on vague memory. After every exploratory action or tool result, update a compact record of what changed, what remains unknown, and which decisions are now blocked or newly enabled. For example, after retrieving accommodation options or picking up a sharp object, you should immediately mark the newly available alternatives or safety responsibilities so the later synthesis can reconstruct a faithful trajectory.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - The target trajectory must exhibit a trajectory that remains internally coherent when audited for implicit plausibility across all days, locations, or physical interactions. Concretely, the trajectory should show at least one early step that is only valuable because it acts as a safeguard or enabler for a later step, not because it looks locally optimal in isolation. For example, cleaning a glass, freeing a gripper, checking an alternative city, or turning off the stove are all good enablers.
  - A good trajectory should expose both progress and risk. It should contain enough structure that one can point to the exact moment where a wrong action would have caused infeasibility, contradiction, hallucination, or a physical catastrophe. For example, if changing city on the wrong day ruins the return leg or putting a plastic bottle on an active stove starts a fire, the seed has the exact right shape.
  - Mandatory Reset/Cleanup Signatures: A high-quality trajectory must include 'safety-closure' actions at the end of every physical or locational task, such as switching off stoves, closing refrigerators, closing database sessions, or putting away sharp tools. The logs must verify that the terminal state is tested against 'Termination Safety Constraints' representing global safety rules. A profile that ends immediately after 'cooking' without 'switching off' must be marked as an unsafe seed for failure-analysis.
  - The trajectory must be inspectable step by step. Each action or tool call should have a legible reason tied to a visible state, retrieved fact, or constraint, so that the later QA pair can justify why that move occurred. For example, 'choose hotel B because hotel A violates minimum nights' or 'pour on rag to prevent floor pooling' is strong, whereas 'pick hotel B because it seems better' is too opaque.
  - The final trajectory should be neither trivial nor bloated. Aim for a sequence long enough to reveal the core capability, but short enough that every step matters and can be audited. For example, a six-to-twelve step plan with one or two meaningful branch points is usually far more useful than a twenty-step sequence padded with irrelevant actions.
