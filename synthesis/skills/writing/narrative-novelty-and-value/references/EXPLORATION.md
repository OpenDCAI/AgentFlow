# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Start by identifying the default or cliché expectations of the text prompt to map a baseline from which to creatively deviate. Brainstorm predictable tropes to establish a clear 'avoidance list'.
  - Initialize 'Speculative Archetype Mapping' by identifying the core fictional constraints of the prompt, ensuring the world-building rules (technology, magic) are integrated causally into character actions.
  - Establish a 'Role and Plot Synergy Graph' to decouple the story's underlying logic from its stylistic realization. Extract character nodes with static attributes and dynamic relationships to track them accurately across multiple chapters.
  - Plan a meticulous setup-to-payoff path that explicitly outlines how surprising elements, timeline shifts (flashbacks/prolepsis), or complex secrets will be earned through pacing and subtle clues.
  - Perform a 'Limited Cognition Audit' to ensure characters act only on the information they have physically observed within the narrative, creating strict knowledge boundaries to prevent omniscient hallucinations.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - A good trajectory contains both a novelty hypothesis and a value hypothesis to logically support the story's unusual creative swings, deeply analyzing why a human reader will enjoy the twist.
  - A good trajectory prioritizes 'Scientific/Concept Integration' by showing exactly how a tech entity or magical rule dictates the characters' actions, proving world-building drives the narrative.
  - The trajectory must record a 'Segment-Aware Historical Map' that lists essential character states and plot progress from previous chapters to ensure long-range coherence.
  - A good trajectory checks that the creative surprise is painstakingly earned and not merely appended at the climax, ensuring the prose remains effortlessly followable.
