# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Clarify the full scope of the long-form request before drafting. Identify the domain, expected audience, requested length, and the breadth-depth tradeoff implied by the task. For example, a 10,000-word historical article can support chronological breadth and analytical depth, while a 4,500-word public report may require narrower scope but more practical detail.
  - Construct a hierarchical plan with explicit section purposes and local budgets. Each major section should answer a distinct subquestion, and the combined plan should cover the whole topic without overlap. An edge case is a long article with many headings that still lacks true breadth because multiple sections restate the same idea.
  - Model dependencies among sections during exploration. Some sections require earlier context to make sense, so the agent should decide the order in which material will be introduced and later referenced. A useful example is defining imperial institutions before discussing their crisis or collapse.
  - Prepare anti-repetition anchors in advance. Long outputs often fail not by missing content but by restating previous material with small variations. A strong exploration step is to assign each section a unique informational role so later drafting can detect when content is bleeding across boundaries.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - A good trajectory shows how the document will accumulate length through planned substance rather than uncontrolled continuation. It should contain section-level or paragraph-level milestones that collectively approach the target. This makes the final output scalable without becoming shapeless.
  - A good trajectory preserves cross-section coherence by carrying forward relevant prior context. Later sections should build on earlier ones instead of restarting the topic from zero. This is why serial writing with access to previously written material is often stronger than fully parallel generation.
  - A good trajectory balances breadth and depth intentionally. It should not cover every subtopic superficially, nor dwell so long on one subtopic that major parts of the topic disappear. An edge case is a history article that spends half its words on the founding era and rushes through centuries of later development.
  - A good trajectory includes explicit repetition checks and transition checks. The agent should know when it is revisiting earlier content and whether that revisit adds genuine synthesis or merely duplicate wording. This is especially important near section boundaries, where summarizing habits often create unnecessary echo.
