# Phase 2: Trajectory Selection Criteria
* **Acceptance Metrics**:
  - Accept a trajectory only if the requested length is supported by a hierarchical plan. The number of sections, their budgets, and their distinct purposes must plausibly add up to the target scale. A trajectory with one vague paragraph plan is not sufficient for a 10,000-word article.
  - Accept a trajectory only if every major section contributes either breadth or depth without redundant overlap. The plan should show what is unique about each section and how the sections connect. This helps prevent long outputs that are numerically long but semantically narrow.
  - Accept a trajectory only if coherence is handled at two levels. Local clarity within sections and global continuity across sections must both be represented in the plan. A good sign is that later sections explicitly depend on concepts introduced earlier.
  - Accept a trajectory only if there is a mechanism for detecting repetition and underdevelopment during generation. The agent should be prepared to compress redundant material and expand under-covered sections as writing progresses. This is crucial when target length pressure can otherwise encourage filler.
* **Rejection Criteria**:
  - Reject any trajectory that aims to satisfy length mainly by repetition or recursive summarization. Long text generated this way may look substantial but lacks authentic breadth and depth. A common failure case is repeatedly restating prior sections under slightly different headings.
  - Reject any trajectory that parallelizes all sections without a strategy for reconciling global coherence. Parallel drafting may improve speed, but it often weakens continuity, transition quality, and consistency of framing. This is especially risky when later sections depend conceptually on earlier ones.
  - Reject any trajectory that overcommits to headings without enough content to justify them. A long outline with many empty or thin sections is a red flag because it encourages shallow padding. An edge case is a report with ten headings where four of them merely rename the introduction.
  - Reject any trajectory that tracks length but not document quality. Hitting the word-count target cannot compensate for low relevance, weak clarity, or missing topic coverage. A draft plan that reaches 4,500 words by bloating definitions while ignoring recommendations should be discarded.
