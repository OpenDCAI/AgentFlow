# Phase 2: Trajectory Selection Criteria
* **Acceptance Metrics**:
  * Accept a trajectory only if the target length has been converted into a clear operational budget. The unit must be explicit and the agent must know whether it is optimizing to a floor, ceiling, or approximate center. For example, a trajectory that says “make it reasonably short” is not acceptable when the prompt says “under 487 words.”
  * Accept a trajectory only if the content plan is compatible with the available budget. Mandatory items must fit without forcing overflow, and long-form plans must include enough substantive material to avoid empty padding. A good example is assigning ten major sections to a 10,000-word article rather than trying to stretch two ideas across ten thousand words.
  * Accept a trajectory only if it contains a live monitoring mechanism. The agent should have at least one explicit checkpoint where current length is compared against the target and the next step is adjusted accordingly. This matters because shortfall and overflow are both predictable before the final line.
  * Accept a trajectory only if length control is decoupled from quality control. The plan should show how relevance, clarity, and coherence will be protected while length is managed. An edge case is a trajectory that promises to “add more examples if too short” but does not define where those examples belong.
* **Rejection Criteria**:
  * Reject any trajectory that relies on last-minute trimming or padding as the primary length-control strategy. This usually produces missing conclusions, abrupt endings, or redundant filler. A classic failure case is a long answer that is chopped to fit a cap without rebalancing sentence flow.
  * Reject any trajectory that confuses one unit of length with another. Words, sentences, paragraphs, and sections are not interchangeable, and the benchmark may verify them differently. For example, hitting 25 sentences does not guarantee compliance if the instruction required 25 words.
  * Reject any trajectory that cannot identify optional material for compression or expansion. Without that buffer, even a small deviation from the target can cause either omission of required content or useless elaboration. This becomes particularly damaging in medium-length tasks where the budget is narrow.
  * Reject any trajectory that reaches target length through repetition, meta-commentary, or re-summarization. The writing may be long enough numerically, but it does not exhibit genuine adherence if the added text does not advance the communicative objective. An edge case is repeatedly restating prior paragraphs in order to push the word count upward.
