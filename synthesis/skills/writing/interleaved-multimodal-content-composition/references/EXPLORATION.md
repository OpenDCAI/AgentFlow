# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Perform a 'Query-to-Evidence Gap Analysis' to identify the specific moments in the planned narrative where a text-only claim is insufficient and requires visual grounding. You must evaluate the user's intent to determine if they need a world map for spatial patterns, a line chart for temporal trends, or a scatter plot for correlations. A concrete step is planning to suspend text generation as soon as a comparative claim (e.g., 'Region A outperforms Region B') is made to ensure a chart is constructed to support it. An edge case involves identifying that a 'Decision-Oriented' report requires a final synthesis dashboard rather than just isolated charts.
  - Execute 'Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) Probing' on raw tables to discover 'Decision-Relevant Insights' before fixing the document outline. You must scan the data for significant figures, patterns, noteworthy exceptions, and outliers (e.g., a country with a 9.5-year gap) that can serve as 'Evidence Anchors' for the narrative. This prevents 'Insight Freezing,' where the report remains at a surface-level summary because the evidence pool was predefined. For example, discovering a correlation between GDP and energy access allows you to plan a section on economic drivers rather than just geographic lists.
  - Map 'Visual-to-Semantic Intent' by pre-defining the required components for every potential visualization request: table subsets, chart types, and specific variables. You must create a 'Visual Construction Plan' that details how each image will support the analysis request, ensuring the chart type (e.g., Violin plot vs. Box plot) is appropriate for the data's distribution. A concrete action is deciding that expressing 'mortality variability' requires a violin plot to show density, whereas 'median comparisons' only require a bar chart. This ensures the constructed visuals are not just decorative but analytically rigorous.
  - Conduct a 'Modal Contextual Alignment Audit' to determine how the prose will reference and interpret the visual evidence. You must plan 'Connective Narrative Bridges'—specific phrasing that refers to visual elements (e.g., 'as shown by the green bar', 'the blue violin is lower')—to ensure the text and chart are mutually reinforcing. For instance, if a chart shows a downward slope, the exploration ledger should record a requirement for the subsequent paragraph to explain the causes of that decline. An edge case involves planning a 'Comparative Bracket' where two charts are placed side-by-side to illustrate a historical shift in energy sources.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - A good trajectory records 'Interleaved Suspension Points' where the agent explicitly identifies the boundary between a textual claim and its required visual proof. It should show the agent noting: 'Claims about the gender gap require spatial visualization; suspending to request World Map [VM1] before proceeding to regional analysis.' This demonstrates a write-time construction logic where the narrative is actively conditioned on emerging empirical evidence.
  - A good trajectory prioritizes 'On-Demand Evidence Construction' over static asset retrieval. It should show the agent interacting with data tables to generate *new* visualization specifications (including filters and aggregations) that specifically answer the current paragraph's question. For example, a trajectory that requests a 'Top 10 vs. Bottom 10' bar chart to illustrate extreme inequality is superior to one that just asks for a 'general table summary.'
  - A good trajectory demonstrates 'High-Fidelity Contextual Weaving,' where the sentences immediately following a visualization tag interpret the chart's findings. It should show the agent extracting a 'Chart-Derived Datum' (e.g., 'Belarus has a 9.33-year gap') from the constructed visual and weaving it directly into the next sentence. A strong indicator is a trajectory that uses the visual's colors or labels (e.g., 'the deep blue shading') to guide the reader's eye toward the core insight.
  - A good trajectory ends with a 'Structural Coherence Pass' that ensures the frequency and variety of visuals match the professional standards of a data-driven report. The agent should verify that it has used a mix of chart types (e.g., maps, bars, lines) and that each visual contributes a unique analytical dimension (Time, Space, or Magnitude). An edge case is a trajectory that rejects a fifth consecutive bar chart in favor of a scatter plot to provide a different statistical perspective on the energy crisis.
