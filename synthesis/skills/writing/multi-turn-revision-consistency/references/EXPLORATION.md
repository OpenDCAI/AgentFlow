# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Perform 'Instruction-to-Edit-Type' classification for the incoming feedback. You must categorize the user's request into 'Content-Growth' (adding new info), 'Format-Shift' (changing structure/style), or 'Correction' (fixing errors) to determine the intervention's footprint. For example, if the feedback is purely stylistic, you must flag the entire existing knowledge-base as a 'Fixed Invariant' that should not be altered. An edge case is 'Self-Reflection' feedback, where you must proactively audit the report against comprehensiveness/factuality rubrics to find your own 'missing targets.'
  - Generate a 'Difference Ledger' by comparing the latest user feedback against the current document state to isolate 'Feedback Targets' vs. 'Stable Content.' You must explicitly list which sections of the Turn 1 report correspond to the feedback and which sections are 'Out-of-Scope' and must be preserved byte-for-byte. A concrete action is creating a list of satisfied 'Checklist Criteria' from earlier turns that are legally 'Protected' from regression. This prevents the common failure where agents over-edit unrelated sections.
  - Perform a 'Citation Continuity Audit' by mapping all existing in-text citation markers to their bibliographic entries before making changes. You must treat these markers as 'Hard Anchors' that must be re-inserted into any revised sentence to avoid citation degradation. If the feedback requires adding *new* facts, search specifically for the additional sources needed without disrupting the URL list for previously established claims. A success here involves ensuring Turn 2's bibliography is a strict superset of Turn 1's, unless a deletion was requested.
  - Analyze 'Multi-Turn Precedence' by reviewing the full thread of previous edits (1, 2, ..., t-1) to ensure the new Turn t does not inadvertently reverse a fix from Turn 1. You must maintain an 'All-History Incorporation' registry to verify that a formatting change in Turn 3 doesn't undo a content addition from Turn 2. An edge case involves recognizing if Turn 3's feedback explicitly 'supersedes' or contradicts Turn 1, requiring you to update your 'Intent Ledger' rather than trying to balance two conflicting goals.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - A high-quality trajectory features a 'Surgical Edit Strategy' that avoids global re-writing in favor of localized delta-application. It should show the agent proposing: 'Only modify Section 2.3 to add the R1 data; keep Sections 1.1, 2.1, and 3.0 frozen to minimize break rate.' This prevents the 'Shapelessness' and content loss seen in single-pass agents. A strong indicator is a trajectory that specifically notes it will 'restore' a conclusion that was nearly deleted during a stylistic shift.
  - A good trajectory documents a 'Structured Edit Plan' before altering a single token. This plan must specify: 1) The Section to be edited, 2) The 'Anchor Quote' (a verbatim phrase from the report) identifying the insertion point, and 3) The 'Constraint Suffix' ensuring only the requested change occurs. This 'PE-fix' (Prompt Engineering) approach dramatically reduces regressions by forcing the agent into a 'Technical Editor' mindset rather than a 'Drafting' mindset. An edge case is identifying an anchor that appears twice and adding a unique identifier to avoid wrong-site editing.
  - A good trajectory demonstrates 'Factual and Presentation Verification' after drafting to ensure no 'Breaks' occurred. It should record a step where the agent compares the Turn 2 checklist coverage against Turn 1 to ensure the 'Oracle Coverage' only increases or stays stable. For instance, the agent should note: 'Turn 1 covered 8 items; Turn 2 now covers 9; verifying that all 8 Turn-1 items are still present in the text.' Any decrease in coverage is flagged as a failure to be corrected.
