# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Execute a 'Treatability/Generalizability Classification' for every identified flaw to determine the appropriate pedagogical response. You must distinguish between 'Generalizable' errors (like subject-verb agreement or pluralization) that follow stable rules and 'Idiosyncratic' errors (like phrasal verbs or specific collocations) that are lexicalized. To do this, check if a rule can be cited to fix the issue; if so, mark it for a hint. A concrete edge case involves a 'missing preposition' that belongs to a fixed phrasal verb, which should be treated as idiosyncratic/untreatable rather than a general grammar rule.
  - Implement a 'SLOWPR Multi-Granular Scan' to evaluate the document across six essential academic dimensions: Structure, Logic, Originality, Writing, Proficiency, and Rigor. You must audit the 'Logic' by checking the alignment between research questions and conclusions, while the 'Rigor' check must focus on citation accuracy and ethical compliance. A specific action involves checking if the 'Originality' of the perspetive reflects independent thinking or just a repetition of the source literature. A failure mode for this strategy is providing a single holistic score without justifying the specific dimension-level strengths and weaknesses.
  - Perform a 'Metalinguistic Knowledge Gap Inference' to identify what specific concept the user is struggling with. You must move past surface-level tags (e.g., 'Wrong Word') to map the error to a professional typology (e.g., 'Possessive Noun' or 'Relative Clause omission'). For instance, if a user writes 'peoples lives,' identify the gap as the concept of possessive markers for plural nouns. This step ensures that the subsequent explanation addresses the 'Why' of the error, not just the 'What.'
  - Perform 'ZPD-Aware Diagnostic Evaluation' by analyzing the difference between what the student can do independently and where they require academic scaffolding. You must look for 'emerging capabilities' in the writing where a student attempts complex reasoning (Logic) or novel solutions (Originality) but lacks the formal 'Proficiency' to execute them perfectly. A concrete step is identifying an 'Axial-Attention' implementation that lacks design motivation; this indicates the student is in the 'Zone of Proximal Development' and needs a specific theoretical hint.
  - Conduct a 'Logic-Gap and Omission Scan' using the target document's rubric or genre standards as a checklist. You must scan the full text for 'Missing Structural Beats' such as a missing research gap in an Introduction or a missing 'Starvation' definition in a CS README. A concrete action is checking every mandatory rubric field against the text and logging any 'Zero-Evidence' nodes as major flaws. An edge case is identifying a 'Motivation' section that exists but is 'belabored,' failing the professional standard for conciseness.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - The trajectory must record a 'Strategic Directness Decision' based on the error's treatability and SLOWPR dimension mapping. It should explicitly state: 'Error is rule-based regarding Rigor/Citation; selecting Indirect Hint strategy to facilitate learning.' This ensures the agent isn't defaulting to lazy 'click-to-fix' behavior for every error type.
  - A good trajectory generates a 'Dimension-Level Rationale Ledger' that justifies every assigned score (Structure, Logic, etc.) with evidence from the text. It should record the agent noting: 'Structure is 9/10 because all standard modules are present; Logic is 8/10 because reasoning is coherent but experimental alignment is slightly loose.' This provides an explicit 'Diagnostic Rationale' that justifies the subsequent hierarchical scoring to the user.
  - The trajectory documents 'Metalinguistic Mapping' between the identified error and a recognized instructional concept like Bloom's Taxonomy levels. It must show the agent selecting a tag like 'Apply' or 'Evaluate' to describe the cognitive failure in the Proficiency or Originality dimensions. For instance, noting that a student failed to 'Evaluate' alternative methods in the Methodology section allows for a more targeted pedagogic suggestion.
  - A good trajectory prioritizes 'Multi-Level Coherence' by ensuring the holistic judgment is a logical aggregation of the six fine-grained scores. It should show a 'Weighting Step' where the agent calculates the final score (e.g., 8.5/10) based on the relative importance of Logic vs. Rigor as defined by the user. An edge case is a trajectory that flags a 'Rigor' failure as so severe that the 'Holistic' score must be penalized regardless of 'Writing' quality.
