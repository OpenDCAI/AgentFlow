# Phase 2: Trajectory Selection Criteria
* **Acceptance Metrics**:
  - The trajectory is acceptable only if the 'Performance Delta' (e.g., lower RMSE, higher reliability) between the initial and evolved workflows is objectively attributable to the recorded optimization steps. You must verify that the improvement in 'Lead Time' or 'Critical Success Index' (CSI) was specifically driven by the mutation of the risk-policy or prompt-template logged in the previous turn. Purely random execution improvements disconnected from the optimization logic strictly warrant rejection.
  - Selected traces must exhibit 'Transparent Auditability,' where the Learning Agent's recommendation explicitly identifies the 'Primary Error Source' (e.g., a radar-shadow bias or a missing safety check) before applying the fix. Reviewers must confirm that the auditor's critique is grounded in the artifacts of the failed run rather than being a generalized Hallucination. A strong case involves an agent stating: 'Phase 1 lacked convective initiation logic, adding Bayesian precursor check now to increase lead time.'
  - The 'Evolutionary Log' must show a discrete 'Calibration Turn' where the system performs a sensitivity analysis on its thresholds (e.g., Testing 80, 90, and 100 mm/h) before committing to a final production policy. You should verify that the orchestrator followed an empirical process to settle on the 'Best' tier configuration. This avoids interactions where the model 'guesses' a new threshold without any supportive trial-and-error reasoning.
  - The agent's optimization must reflect 'Strategic Resilience,' meaning the evolved workflow successfully handles a subsequent environmental disruption that the original baseline could not survive. The trace must prove that the 'Adaptation Gain' (M5) is functionally active, such as a routing agent successfully maintaining 81% viable routes in a storm compared to a baseline of 68%. This ensures the data trains for active operational resilience rather than just text superficiality.
* **Rejection Criteria**:
  - Reject any trajectory where the 'Optimization' is simulated via a human user editing the prompt or manually telling the agent 'you were wrong about the threshold'. The evolution MUST be autonomous, performed by an internal Learning and Audit Layer reading outcome logs. Human-in-the-loop guidance is a separate skill; evolutionary optimization requires the system to be the 'actor-network' that self-corrects based on environment feedback.
  - Discard traces where the 'Evolved Policy' is too aggressive or too passive, leading to a catastrophic spike in False Alarms (FAR) or a total lack of utility. If an agent lowers a warning threshold to 0mm/h to ensure it 'never misses a flood,' it has failed the multi-objective balancing requirement. High-quality Refinement must maintain a balance between early detection and operational readiness.
  - Reject trajectories where the 'performance gain' is claimed rhetorically but never empirically proven through a subsequent 'Verification Phase' that uses the same ground-truth metrics as the baseline. The trajectory must show a final 'Test' step verifying the modified workflow accomplishes the task that the baseline originally failed. Without closed-loop validation (M3), the optimization remains an ungrounded hypothesis.
  - Reject any trace where the optimizer 'cheats' by finding the ground truth answer or the 'event label' in its memory and embedding it directly into the prompt as a hard-coded solution. The mutation must be generalized sufficiently to solve future, non-identical scenarios (e.g., evolving a general 'Disaster Triage' logic from a specific 'Buner Flood' log). Hard-coding session-specific results into the policy violates the meta-learning purpose of the skill.
