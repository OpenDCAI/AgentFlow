# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Implement 'Cinematographic Joint-Visibility Auditing' to classify the shot scale (Close-Up, Medium, Full-Body) by analyzing the lowest visible human joint in the frame buffer. You must identify the lower boundary of the human bounding box and cross-reference it with keypoints: if only shoulders/head are visible, log it as 'Close-up (CU)'; if hips are the cut-off, log it as 'Medium Shot (MS)'; and if ankles are visible, log it as 'Full Shot (FS)'. For example, if a speaker is gesturing at 15s and the frame cuts exactly at their knees, you must record this as a 'Medium Full Shot (MFS)'.
  - Execute 'Synchronized Emotional-Structural Correlation' to map vocal intensity markers and script narrative changes to the exact second of camera transitions. You should monitor the audio track for shifts in pitch, pace, or tone (e.g., transitioning from calm to raised intensity) that logically prompt a director to 'cut' to a tighter shot. A concrete instance involves logging a 500ms audio 'emphasized' token immediately followed by a visual transition to a Close-up, establishing the causal intent for the structural edit.
  - Conduct 'Temporal Transition Pixel-Delta Profiling' to distinguish between camera pans (continuous motion) and structural cuts (discontinuous jumps). You must analyze the frame-to-frame similarity between T and T+1; if the background geometry and subject depth reset instantly without a kinetic bridge, document this as a 'Smash Cut' boundary. For instance, notice the zero-pixel overlap when a wide stage view suddenly leaps to a tight head-shot, proving the existence of a definitive structural gap.
  - Perform 'Boundary Point Process Arrival-Modeling' to track the frequency and distribution of shot changes across a long-form montage. You should consistently log the intervals between every camera switch to determine if the editing pace accelerates during climax segments (e.g., cuts every 2s) or slows during expositions. A successful trace maps the temporal density of arrivals, allowing the agent to forecast the next likely transition based on established cinematic rhythms.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - The explored trajectory must feature 'Cinematographic Scale Divergence' where the identified boundary represents a definitive change in the visual shot type (e.g., from Wide Shot to Close-up). You must ensure that the transition is not just a same-scale jump cut but a purposeful shift in perspective that demands a re-classification of the subject's visible body parts. An ideal trajectory logs a clear change from a 'head and shoulders' view to a 'waist-up' view at the boundary frame.
  - The trajectory must support 'Emotive-Structural Grounding' demonstrating that the temporal localization of the cut is inextricably linked to a multimodal cue from the speech or audio. You must verify that the cut occurs at a narrative pivot point, such as the end of a sentence or a shift in vocal pitch as observed in the 'TalkCuts' methodology. If the camera cuts during silence without a visual or audio trigger, the trajectory lacks the necessary 'reasoning depth' for high-quality synthesis.
  - Maintain 'Frame-Exact Boundary Stability' ensuring that the onset and offset numerical limits for a shot type are locked to the absolute first and last frames of the edit sequence. You must establish localized anchoring metrics strictly governed by the first frame where the body-part visibility changes. An excellent localization path dictates a timestamp linked precisely to the pixel-reset of the camera rather than a general 1-second window surrounding the event.
  - The path must incorporate 'Multi-Perspective Continuity' verifying that the speaker's identity and orientation are logically preserved across the temporal gap. You must document that although the shot type changes (e.g., from side-view to front-view), the primary entity remains identical in attribute and narrative role. If the subject teleports or changes clothes across the cut, the trajectory must flag a potential identity-drift failure.
