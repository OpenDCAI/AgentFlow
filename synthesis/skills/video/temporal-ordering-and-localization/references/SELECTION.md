# Phase 2: Trajectory Selection Criteria
* **Acceptance Metrics**:
  - Mandate exactly one 'Shot-Type Classification Milestone' where the agent successfully labels the segment using a defined cinematographic scale (CU, MCU, MS, MFS, FS, or WS). You must verify the classification by counting visible whole-body joints (head, torso, hips, knees, ankles) in the representative frames of the segment. A sample is accepted only if the shot-type label matches the observed visibility constraints with 100% geometric accuracy.
  - Require 'Boundary Intersection Precision' where the identified cut timestamp deviates from the ground truth edit by no more than 33 milliseconds (approx. one frame at 30fps). You should deploy a proximity sweep to ensure the agent didn't generalize the transition over a multi-second fade or dissolve. Accept the sample only if the 'Zero-Point' of the hard cut is isolated as the rigid temporal threshold.
  - Enforce 'Narrative-Boundary Logical Convergence' where the relationship between the shot transition and the speech content follows a single, interpretable intent (e.g., zooming in because the speaker is whispering). You must verify that the reasoning (e.g., Shot A transitioned to Shot B because of vocal emphasis at T=10) is based on explicit multimodal evidence. For example, reject samples where the camera shot changes randomly without any corresponding change in action or dialogue.
  - Verify 'Geometric Scale Contrast' ensuring that the two segments separated by the boundary possess a measurable difference in human bounding-box frame occupancy. You must calculate the 'Subject-to-Frame' ratio; if Segment A the head takes 10% of height and in Segment B it takes 80%, the cut is a valid 'Scale Transition'. This ensures the task evaluates structural understanding rather than trivial sequence identification.
* **Rejection Criteria**:
  - Immediately reject 'Static-View / Single-Shot Loops' which feature long durations without any cinematographic editing or shot-scale variation. You should systematically purge videos where the camera remains at a fixed medium-shot without transitions, as these provide zero 'Structural Analysis' value. The required temporal deduction must hinge on identifying the specific moment the perspective or scale shifts.
  - Discard any trajectory exhibiting 'Unresolvable Boundary Occlusion' where the camera switches, but the subject is obscured by a prop or lighting failure during the first 5 frames of the new shot. You must verify that the 'Zero-Point' of the new shot type is identifiable via clear pixels. If the transition results in a blurry or dark frame that prevents identifying the shot scale (e.g., CU vs MS), the sample is rejected.
  - Eliminate 'Artifact-Driven Cut Illusions' falsely labeling standard video encoding lag, screen flickering, or transmission glitches as intentional cinematographic transitions. You must inspect the proposed boundary verifying that the pixel reset is a clean, purposeful directorial edit rather than a 404-error or codec failure. Proper structural analysis demands distinguishing between a 'Smash Cut' and a 'Technical Glitch'.
  - Reject trajectories that rely on 'Subtitle/Transcript Spoilers' where narrator commentary or instruction overlays (like '[Laughter]' or '[Shot change]') explicitly state the transition type. You must perform an OCR and transcript ablation; if the answer 'it is a close-up' is only known because a text tag says 'CLOSE UP', the visual structural task is bypassed. The agent must deduce the shot type from the pixel distribution of human body parts.
