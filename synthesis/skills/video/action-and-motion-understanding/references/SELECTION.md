# Phase 2: Trajectory Selection Criteria
* **Acceptance Metrics**:
  - Mandate an 'Action-Unit Density Threshold' where the clip must contain at least 3 discrete, identifiable kinetic units per 5 seconds of footage to qualify for 'high-frequency' analysis. First, count the number of racquet-ball or hand-object interactions logged in Phase 1. If the video is largely 'dead time' (e.g., walking between points), discard the sample as it lacks the required information density for motion complex-reasoning.
  - Require 'Physical Impact Clarity' ensuring the point of contact between the agent and object is visible for at least 3 continuous frames. You must verify that motion blur is not so severe that the racquet and ball merge into an indistinguishable pixel cloud. Reliable hit-direction inference requires seeing the racquet face angle at the moment of peak compression.
  - Enforce 'Monotonic Rally Progression' checks where the recorded strike sequence follows an alternating A-B-A-B pattern in competitive sports scenarios. First, check the identity tags of the strikers; if the trajectory notes show 'Player A' hitting twice in a row without a bounce or opponent touch, the sample is likely a tracking error and should be rejected. This safeguards logical sports-physics consistency.
  - Verify 'Geometric Boundary Integrity' ensuring the entire field of play (e.g., court, ring, pitch) is sufficiently visible to confirm the direction and result of a maneuver. You must confirm that at least 70% of the ball's trajectory is visible post-impact. A sample is retained only if the agent can definitively mark where the ball 'lands' relative to the visible court lines without interpolating off-screen zones.
* **Rejection Criteria**:
  - Immediately reject 'Generic Cyclic Habit Samples' where the motion is repetitive with no consequence or technical variation (e.g., bouncing a ball on a racquet for 20 seconds). These samples provide zero 'task' or 'tactical' reasoning value as they lack the intent-outcome bridge. We prioritize clips where each action unit contributes to a singular global outcome like a 'point won' or a 'miss'.
  - Discard any trajectory exhibiting 'Temporal Sampling Gap Failure' where a strike occurs during a frame drop or black-out window. First, audit the timeline for missing frames between the player's swing onset and the ball's exit. If the actual impact frame is missing (leading to the ball 'teleporting' across the racquet), the sample is rejected as it prevents accurate action recognition.
  - Eliminate 'Categorical Difficulty Shortcuts' where the ranking of tasks is based on labels (e.g., 'Tennis vs Golf') rather than actual kinetic Action Units within the clip. We reject queries where the answer can be found in common-sense knowledge (e.g., 'running is harder than walking') without analyzing the unique speed or effort deltas visible in the specific video. The deduction must be derived from the pixels.
  - Reject trajectories relying on 'Narrator Overlays or Scoreboard Spoilers' where the hit count or outcome is explicitly displayed as text. You must check for OCR-capable regions on the screen; if the rally count is listed in the broadcast's digital UI, the visual comprehension task is bypassed. We demand 'Raw Pixel Reasoning' where the agent deduces the count from the movement, not the transcript.
