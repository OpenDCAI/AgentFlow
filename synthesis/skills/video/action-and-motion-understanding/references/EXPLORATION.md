# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Implement High-Density Temporal Windowing for short, information-dense sports clips where actions happen in sub-second intervals. You must increase sampling density to at least 10-15 frames per second during active interaction bursts to avoid missing racquet-ball impact points or footwork pivots. For example, when exploring a tennis serve, focus the extraction effort on the 300ms window where the ball reaches its peak height to capture the exact strike frame.
  - Execute 'Atomic Entity-Interaction Mapping' to independently log every instance where an agent makes physical contact with an object. You should document the onset and offset of each kinetic event (e.g., 'racquet contact start' to 'ball release') to build a chronological sequence of 'hit' units. An edge case involves ignoring 'air swings' or 'shadow swings' where the motion exists but the interaction with the target object (the ball) is zero.
  - Perform 'Joint-Pivot and Vector Analysis' to distinguish between similar-looking athletic techniques based on subtle biomechanical cues. You must trace the angular shifts in the elbow, shoulder, and wrist during the execution phase to clarify if a shot is a slice, topspin, or flat hit. For instance, documenting a downward racquet trajectory relative to the ball indicates a slice, which serves as a foundational observation for technique classification.
  - Conduct 'Trajectory-Exit Tracking' to verify the direction and outcome of high-speed object movements immediately following a kinetic impact. You should monitor the ball or projectile's path for the first 10 frames after contact to determine its global orientation (Cross-court vs. Down-the-line). A failure to track the exit vector often leads to misidentifying the outcome (In/Out) because the agent loses the link between the action and its physical consequence.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - A good trajectory must satisfy 'Action-Level Determinacy' where every calculated tally or classification is backed by a specific frame-level impact event. First, verify that your notes provide a 1-to-1 mapping between a 'Count' and an observed interaction frame. For example, a rally count of 4 must have 4 distinct 'impact' timestamps logged, proving the answer is not a statistical guess.
  - The explored path must demonstrate 'Momentum Sensitivity' distinguishing between a purposeful kinetic strike and a passive equipment bounce. You must verify that the agent's velocity (e.g., racquet speed) is increasing or maintaining force at the point of contact. This ensures that a ball accidentally hitting a player is not counted as a 'stroke action' in sports-reasoning tasks.
  - Ensure the path established 'Sequential Technique Consistency' where the recognized technique is physically possible given the player's previous orientation. First, document the 'Preparation Phase' where the player moves into position; a forehand stroke must be preceded by a move that puts the ball on the dominant-hand side of the body. If the player is running left but hits a right-handed volley, flag the trajectory for identity-lock re-verification.
  - The trajectory must accommodate 'Outcome-Boundary Grounding' by capturing the ball's final resting place or its traversal across the net. First, ensure the trace follows the ball until it either bounces twice, hits the net, or lands outside the perimeter. Documenting the specific frame the ball crosses the net plane is critical for validating 'Hit Outcome' reasoning.
