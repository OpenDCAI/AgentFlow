# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Start by mining raw video regions that foreground videos with multiple consequential events, long temporal span, and a natural need for selective compression or narrative evolution tracking. First, scan a candidate video for the exact moment where the answer becomes recoverable and mark the earliest, decisive, and latest clue windows. For example, in a long vlog, log 'T=0-10: Packing', 'T=10-40: Driving', and 'T=40-60: Hiking' to build a logical skeleton before diving into details.
  - Implement Narrative Anchor-Point Indexing to map the high-level structural milestones of the long video, such as shifts in location, tone, or changes in the central activity. First, gather at least one nearby distractor segment in which the same subject appears but the temporal context changes the outcome. For example, keep two nearly identical kitchen clips if one ends a storyline differently.
  - Conduct Inter-Event Transitional Logic Mapping specifically to identify the visual or auditory signals that bridge two distinct episodes, even across smash cuts. First, locate the Offset frame of Event A and the Onset frame of Event B, then search for Linking Cues. For instance, in a rapidly edited trailer, document the visual/thematic link carrying the character from a chaotic street directly to a quiet room.
  - Annotate trajectory notes while exploring instead of reconstructing them later, ensuring a continuous thread of the Main Objective is tracked. First, record the minimal sequence of pivot observations that caused the narrative to shift, tying each to a temporal location. For example, write 'at 45m the character changes their plan due to the weather' rather than 'plan changed'.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - A good trajectory must require integrating separated moments rather than paraphrasing the opening scene or the title alone, and must satisfy Sequence Dependency. First, verify that if Event A is removed, Event B appears irrational or unexplained. For example, the reason a character is 'happy' at 50m should only be known by retrieving the 'success' event from 10m.
  - A good trajectory must establish Thematic Continuity by linking entities and objectives across disparate timestamps with zero identity drift. First, ensure your trajectory notes use a standardized Protagonist-ID throughout a long or disjointed stream. For example, if evidence only supports 'something with the car' but not 'repairing the engine', the storyline is still under-specified.
  - A good trajectory must naturally lead to a coherent chronological summary that preserves outcome, pivot transitions, and plot evolution. First, document at least 3 to 5 intermediate Pivot Points where the state of the task or character definitively shifted. For example, a reviewer should be able to follow the phase shifts and confirm the final plot consequence without inventing extra steps.
  - A good trajectory must survive adversarial shortcut checks and Static Snapshot Ablation. First, verify that the end state could not have been predicted from the 50% mark of the video due to high uncertainty. For example, if a team is debating a plan, the trajectory must show the specific moment the decision was made, as the final frame alone shows them already in action.
