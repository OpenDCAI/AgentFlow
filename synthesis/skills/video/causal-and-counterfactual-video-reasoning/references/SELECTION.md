# Phase 2: Trajectory Selection Criteria
* **Acceptance Metrics**:
  - Mandate exactly one 'Anomaly Perception Gap' where identifying the event as a crime or danger is impossible through static image analysis or label-guessing alone. First, simulate a 'Context-Free' check; if a single frame shows a man with a bag, but the 10-second video shows him 'Stealing' it, the sample is accepted. This ensures the reasoning is earned by tracking the causal evolution of the behavior rather than simple object detection.
  - Require 'Bi-Directional Role Confirmation' where the interaction between the 'Perpetrator' and the 'Target/Victim' is clearly defined via kinetic contact or property change. First, evaluate the 'Interaction Delta'—if one person hits and the other recoils or flees, the roles are verified. We prioritize samples where 'Assault' or 'Fighting' is proven by the antagonistic physical response of at least two participating agents.
  - Enforce 'Ground-Truth Recoverability' where the anomaly classification (e.g., Arson, Robbery, Shoplifting) is uniquely supported by the visual and auditory evidence without narrator spoilers. First, check for 'Direct Visual Evidence' like a lighter being used on a curtain (Arson) or a door being pried open (Burglary). A sample passes if a human observer, provided with the same clip, would reach the same classification using legal/social common sense.
  - Verify 'Resolution Fidelity' ensuring that the final outcome (arrested, transported, escaped) is visually confirmed within the clip or its immediate aftermath. First, verify that the 'suspect' is still in view when the 'Arrest' or 'Neutralization' milestones occur. An accepted sample shows the final step of the causal chain, such as the suspect being 'placed on a stretcher', which confirms the event's gravity and outcome.
* **Rejection Criteria**:
  - Immediately reject 'Generic Activity Shortcuts' where social norms are not actually violated or the anomaly is trivially explained by standard habits. First, discard mundane events like 'police walking on patrol' if no crime occurs, as this lacks the 'Anomaly Deduction' requirement. We aggressively filter out 'Normal' videos that offer nothing for the agent to audit or classify as a conflict/hazard.
  - Discard any candidate trajectory suffering from 'Intent Ambiguity' where it is unclear if an action is a crime or a normal accident without further evidence. First, test the 'Evidence Confidence'; if a person falls and it's unclear if they were pushed or just tripped, discard the sample. We demand 'Evidence-Based Anomaly Perception' where the criminal trigger (e.g., a push, a hidden weapon) is distinctly visible and predictable.
  - Eliminate 'Disjointed Anomaly Samples' where the crime (e.g., a shooting) and the suspect are never shown in the same logical narrative or camera view. First, check for 'Causal Connection'; if the camera shows a shattered window but never shows a person or a projectile, the cause is unresolvable. These loops fail the requirement to 'trace' intent and must be purged in favor of contiguous action-consequence sequences.
  - Reject samples relying on 'Caption/Metadata Leaks' where the video title or a text overlay explicitly states 'ROBBERY IN PROGRESS' before the visual event is earned. First, perform an 'OCR/Text Audit'; if the video's digital overlay provides the classification, the cognitive interpretation task is bypassed. Causal classification must be derived solely from the interpretation of sub-second kinetic and social cues.
