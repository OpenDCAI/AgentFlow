# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Implement 'Dynamic Anomaly-Trigger Scanning' to identify the exact second a routine social scene mutates into a conflict or crime. First, the agent must define a 'Normalcy Baseline' for the specific environment, such as people walking or shopping peacefully. Then, it monitors for sudden 'Kinetic Spikes' or 'Social Norm Deviations' like running, shouting gestures, or concealed item handling. A concrete example includes logging a person in a store who suddenly puts an item in their pocket instead of a basket, marking the T-start of a potential Shoplifting event.
  - Execute 'Crime-Scene Entity and Role Scaffolding' to categorize all actors as Perpetrators, Victims, or Bystanders based on their interaction vectors. First, identify the subject who initiates a non-consensual physical act or property state-change. Next, track the 'Recipient' of the action and any nearby individuals who react (e.g., fleeing or intervening). For instance, in an 'Assault' video, the agent logs the individual throwing the first punch as the primary perpetrator and those running away as bystanders to map the social causality.
  - Perform 'Multimodal Contradiction Auditing' to detect anomalies where visual kinesthetics or audio signals conflict with expected 'Safe' behavior. You must first analyze the audio for 'Distress Cues' like screaming, sirens, or breaking glass even if they occur off-screen. Then, correlate these with visual environmental shifts such as smoke (Arson) or crowd dispersal. An edge case involves a crowd appearing to 'exercise' when a closer look at the audio/facial cues reveals they are actually 'Fighting' or involved in a 'Riot'.
  - Conduct 'Environmental Safety and Policy-Violation Mapping' by proactively auditing for breaches in social or legal codes within the frame. You should first establish the 'Prescriptive Rules' of the zone, such as 'No Trespassing' or 'Standard Traffic Flow'. Then, scan for objects or behaviors that violate these rules, such as a car driving the wrong way (Road Accident) or a person entering a window (Burglary). A failure to identify the window-entry as 'Burglary' rather than 'Climbing' represents a failure in normative causal deduction.
  - Deploy 'Causal-Chain Outcome Extraction' to track the resolution and consequence phase of an anomaly, such as the arrival of police or medical teams. First, identify the 'Resolution Trigger' (e.g., someone calling for help or an alarm sounding). Then, track the sequence of the suspect's final state—whether they are arrested, flee, or are neutralized. For example, explicitly log that a suspect who was initially fighting is ultimately 'transported via stretcher' to provide a complete causal account of the incident.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - The explored trajectory must feature 'Normative Saliency' where the core event is an undisputed violation of standard environmental safety or social policy. First, verify that the anomaly (e.g., robbery, shoplifting) is not ambiguous or a false positive like a staged play or a joke. A high-quality path will show clear visual evidence of a crime—such as a weapon being drawn or an item being forcefully taken—which provides the grounding for a causal 'Guilty' verdict.
  - Maintain 'Identity-Locked Role Consistency' throughout the entire duration of the anomalous event and its resolution. First, ensure the 'Perpetrator' identified at the onset of the crime is the same individual being tracked during the arrest or flight phase. If the agent loses the target during a crowd surge and starts tracking a bystander as the 'thief', the trajectory is discarded due to identity-drift causing a false causal attribution.
  - A good trajectory must support 'Temporal Boundary Determinacy' allowing for the exact extraction of onset (the first illegal/unsafe act) and offset (the resolution/conclusion). First, the agent must document the 'Point of No Return' frame where a situation transitions from 'Suspicious' to 'Criminal'. For example, in a Burglary, the onset is precisely when the window glass breaks, not when the person is first seen outside.
  - Ensure the path contains 'Explicit Causal Justification' linking the visual evidence to the intent or internal state of the agents (Theory of Mind). First, verify that the perpetrator's actions (e.g., looking around nervously, wearing a mask) provide enough context to infer 'Criminal Intent'. If a person accidentally breaks a window and apologizes, but the agent labels it 'Vandalism' without checking the reaction, the causal logic is insufficient.
