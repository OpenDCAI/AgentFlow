# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Implement 'Safety-Landmark Anchor-Point Indexing' to identify critical zones where adherence to protocols is most strictly measured. You must first scan the video for professional landmarks—such as the Calot triangle in surgery or the grounding wire in electrical work—marking these as 'High-Stake Regions'. A concrete instance involves focusing on the exact frames where a specific 'Target Structure' is being cleared of debris to ensure the safety criteria can be accurately scored.
  - Execute 'Multi-Phase Sequential Boundary Mapping' to divide long trajectories into discrete work phases based on tool use or environmental shifts. You should identify the 'Trigger Action' (e.g., picking up a grasper) that signifies the start of a sub-task and the 'Release Action' that completes it. For example, in a complex repair, you must log when the diagnostic phase ends and the intervention phase begins to correctly answer 'What was the previous phase?' queries.
  - Initiate 'Expert Rubric Alignment' by pre-defining a set of scoring dimensions (e.g., respect for surroundings, efficient motion, flow of operation) before scanning the video. You should assign a preliminary mental score to each action as it occurs, logging evidence such as 'abrupt movement at T=45s' to justify a lower 'Flow of Operation' rating. An edge case involves tracking 'idle time' where an agent is inactive, which serves as negative evidence for 'Time and Motion' efficiency scores.
  - Conduct 'Protocol-to-Visual Synchronization' to explicitly align the observed workflow with rules extracted from provided external manuals or safety checklists. You should establish an abstract list of required behaviors or tools before initiating the scan, logging each corresponding visual confirmation along the video timeline. A concrete instance involves searching exclusively for 'safety goggles' or 'double-gloves' at the commencement of a hazardous procedure because domain guidance mandates it.
  - Execute 'Inter-Phase Transitional Logic Auditing' to identify the exact second a routine task progresses to a critical decision point. You must monitor for 'Decision Cues'—like an operator pausing to inspect a structure or a machine beep—that signal a transition in logic. For instance, in a medical context, you log the transition from 'Dissection' to 'Aspiration' to ensure the sequential summary preserves the narrative evolution of the procedure.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - A high-quality trajectory must satisfy 'Normative Saliency' where the identified boundary or scoring decision represents a definitive adherence to or violation of a standard protocol. First, ensure the event (e.g., skipping a step, achieving a safety milestone) is not ambiguous and can be verified by 1-to-1 mapping to professional rules. For example, a high-quality path highlights the explicit achievement of 'cystic plate exposure' rather than just a general view of the liver.
  - Maintain 'Analytical Consistency' throughout the temporal duration to ensure that performance metrics are not biased by early or late impressions. You must verify that the 'Skill Level' assigned at the end is justified by a continuous thread of observations across all segments. If an operator starts with 'Expert' precision but makes a 'Novice' error at 10m, the trajectory must capture this shift in sequential logic to justify the final assessment.
  - The explored path must support 'Procedural Grounding Verifiability' indicating that any alleged mistake or violation unambiguously contravenes an indexed rule or physical standard. You must capture clear kinetic evidence demonstrating the flawed execution in opposition to the established task logic. An explicit case involves recording a worker handling a specimen without the correct bag, confirming the violation is visually measurable rather than ideologically assumed.
  - Ensure the path demonstrates 'Chronological Monotonicity' leaving zero inexplicable temporal gaps within the critical path of the procedure. You must ensure the progression chain remains unbroken, documenting transitional handoffs separating discrete procedural units. A successful extraction traces a full assembly timeline smoothly from loose component parts to the finalized frame without omitting critical connective steps.
