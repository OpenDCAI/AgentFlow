# Phase 2: Trajectory Selection Criteria
* **Acceptance Metrics**:
  - Mandate exactly one 'Decision Point Evaluation' where identifying the outcome or score relies on correlating the tracked history to a professional protocol. You must verify whether forecasting the next action is causally linked to identifying a completed safety milestone. A technical sample succeeds if the agent predicts 'Cutting' only after it has documented 'Two-structure confirmation' at a specific timestamp.
  - Require 'Expert-Level Precision Threshold' where descriptions of actions use domain-specific technical terms rather than lay language. You must evaluate the exploration notes to ensure and verify that tools and tissues are named exactly (e.g., 'Maryland dissector' vs 'pliers'). Accept the sample only if the 'Action Precision' captures the specific technical nuance—like 'coagulating' instead of 'burning'.
  - Enforce 'Safety-Metric Saliency' demanding that any non-compliance or achievement of a safety standard registers clearly on pixel-level metrics without reliance on voiceovers. You must inspect the specific frame anomaly, accepting the trace only if the procedural status (e.g., a wheal forming during an injection) presents a high-resolution geometric footprint. For example, a 'full clearance' milerstone must be distinctly visible as clear space behind a structure.
  - Verify 'Multi-Dimensional Score Distribution' for skill assessment tasks, ensuring that the trajectory provides evidence for at least 3 distinct scoring variables. You must check that the notes cover independent features like 'Time and Motion', 'Tool Handling', and 'Final Product Quality'. A valid performance audit requires at least one specific visual observation cited for each of the reported scores to prevent 'score inflation' or generic rating.
* **Rejection Criteria**:
  - Immediately reject 'Generic Activity Shortcuts' containing trivial daily habits or generic tropes where the sequential ordering bypasses technical necessity. You should systematically purge inquiries asking 'what happens after opening a door' where the answer is globally intuitive independent of professional video logic. The required sequential deduction must hinge on unpatterned visual evidence unique to that specific professional workflow.
  - Discard any trajectory exhibiting 'Score Inconsistency' where the final numeric rating (e.g., 4/5) contradicts the logged visual evidence (e.g., 'frequent fumbling'). First, audit the relationship between descriptors and scores; if the description cites 'perfect execution' but the score is '1/5', discard the sample. This protects the agent from generating data based on hallucinated assessments that do not match the physical reality.
  - Eliminate 'No-Logic / Perfect-Execution Loop Samples' consisting of uneventful cyclical motions entirely devoid of critical decision points or safety milestones. You should analyze if the video is merely a repetitive background loop lacking internal task milestones. A static video of an automated machine moving continuously provides zero procedural decision reasoning and must be rejected.
  - Reject trajectories that rely heavily on 'Subtitle or Overlay Spoilers' where narrator commentary or scoreboards explicitly state the upcoming phase or the final skill score. You must conduct a transcript ablation confirming the physical kinetic cues independently drive the assessment. If a digital overlay says 'Phase: Dissection' or 'Score: 10/10', the visual comprehension and procedural mapping task is bypassed.
