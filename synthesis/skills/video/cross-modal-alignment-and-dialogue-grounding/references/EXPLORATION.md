# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Implement 'Six-Directional Basic Subtask Indexing' to systematically map relations between Time (T), Video content (V), and Audio content (A). You should independently log V2T, T2V, A2T, T2A, V2A, and A2V vectors to ensure the knowledge base supports any bi-directional query path. For example, if you find a man speaking at T=10s, you must record the speech text, the visual action of his lips/gestures, and the absolute timestamps as a unified triplet.
  - Execute 'Audiovisual Temporal Interleaved Representation' by sampling visual frames and audio segments at synchronized millisecond intervals. You must interleave the text-based timestamp (e.g., second{2.0}), the frame tokens, and the raw audio feature tokens into a sequential timeline buffer. This prevents the modalities from drifting, ensuring that a 'sizzling' sound detected at T=5.0s is linked strictly to the 'pan' visual at that exact index.
  - Implement 'Phonetic-Visual Anchor Mapping' to identify segments where the audio transcript contains low-confidence tokens that might match on-screen text. You should flag proper nouns or technical terms in the ASR transcript that have low probability scores. Then, perform a targeted OCR scan of the surrounding visual frames. For example, if the audio says 'NextSeat' but a slide shows 'Nexeed', you must log this as a corrected anchor point.
  - Conduct 'Modality-Specific Detailed Annotation' by extracting separate, exhaustive prose descriptions for the visual and auditory streams to test for Cross-Modal Contradictions. You must describe visual attributes and auditory attributes independently. If you discover an explicit disagreement (e.g., a narrator says 'the trend is increasing' but the chart line drops), document this 'High-Bias Zone' for later cross-comparison.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - A good trajectory must satisfy 'Mutual Modality Necessity' where the query is unsolvable using a single stream or text alone. You must verify that a 'muted-video' or 'black-screen' baseline would lead to semantic ambiguity for the specific question. For example, if the question asks for the color of a spatula during a specific speech, the trajectory is high-quality because the color is only in the video and the timing is only in the audio.
  - The trajectory must feature 'Corrective Modality Interdependence' where a final fact is derived by intentionally overriding a noisy signal in one stream using a clear signal in another. First, verify that the 'Raw ASR' or 'Raw Vision' contained an ambiguity that was successfully fixed. For example, the trajectory should show the agent identifying 'Bisque' in the audio and 'Bisk' on a slide, ultimately documenting 'Bisk'.
  - Maintain 'Interleaved Chronological Monotonicity' where all sampled triplets follow a strictly forward-moving timeline with absolute second markers. You must ensure every observation uses a standardized format like 'second{t}' to avoid frame-index confusion between different frame-rate clips. For instance, your notes should align 'second{113.2}' with both the 'pan' visual and the 'sausage' audio cue.
  - Ensure 'Symbolic Modality Disentanglement' by verifying that the audio description does not use visual-only words and the video description does not use audio-only words. You must audit your exploration notes to ensure the audio segment caption stays strictly in the auditory domain (e.g., 'resonant boom'). This protects the synthesis phase from providing 'leaked' answers.
