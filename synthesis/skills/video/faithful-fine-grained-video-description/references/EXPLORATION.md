# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Implement Atomic Event Bracketing to break the entire video into discrete, verifiable milestones of visual, auditory, and textual information. To do this, scrub through the timeline and log every single unique sentence spoken, every action lasting more than 0.5 seconds, and every instance of on-screen text as a standalone 'unit'. For example, if a character picks up a glass while a subtitle appears, you must record 'T=1: Action (Pick up glass)' and 'T=1: Text (Dialogue Subtitle)' separately before synthesis.
  - Execute Dual-Modality Corroboration by cross-referencing visual cues with audio segments to ensure holistic event mapping. You should explicitly check if a sound (e.g., a door slam) corresponds to a visible action and note the synchronization or any off-screen occurrences. A concrete edge case involves a dog barking off-camera; you must document the audio as a prompt to search for the dog in subsequent frames to maintain narrative continuity.
  - Perform Hallucination-Trigger Auditing by identifying background regions or poor-lighting segments where standard models often 'invent' details. You must perform a high-zoom scan on peripheral entities (like books on a shelf or people in the background) to determine if they are identifiable or if they should be labeled as 'blurry/unrecognizable' objects to prevent generic guessing. For instance, if you see a blurry logo, document it as 'red circular logo' rather than 'Coca-Cola' unless the text is explicitly verified by OCR.
  - Conduct Holistic Information Scaffolding by integrating OCR labels and metadata into the chronological event log. First, identify all static and scrolling text overlays, watermarks, and user interface elements that provide context like location or time. An example is logging a 'Live in New York' tag to anchor the description's setting, ensuring that the synthesized description uses these technical anchors rather than subjective visual estimates.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - The trajectory must support an Atomic Event Density of at least 10 unique milestones per minute of video to ensure sufficient descriptive granularity. To verify this, count the total number of logged visual actions, spoken facts, and text appearaces in your notes. A low-density trajectory (e.g., only noting the start and end) must be discarded as it fails the completeness requirement of a fine-grained description.
  - A high-quality trajectory must satisfy Modal Interdependence by containing at least 20% 'non-visual' clues such as speech transcriptions or environmental sound markers. You must ensure that your notes do not rely exclusively on the frame stream, as this leads to 'Missing Event' errors regarding narrator intent or off-screen context. For example, a video of a lecture must have the key points of the speech logged as the primary skeleton for the visual transitions.
  - The path must maintain Narrative Factual Consistency by verifying that entities identified at the start are the same entities at the end through persistent attribute tracking. First, establish a 'Digital ID' for every person or object and check their traits (clothing color, hair style) every 10 seconds to detect scene cuts or splices. If a 'man in blue' suddenly appears as a 'man in green' without a costume-change event, the trajectory must flag a potential splice or tracking failure.
