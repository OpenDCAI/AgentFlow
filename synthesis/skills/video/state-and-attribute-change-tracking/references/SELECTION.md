# Phase 2: Trajectory Selection Criteria
* **Acceptance Metrics**:
  - Require a 'Dynamic Magnitude Score' greater than a baseline threshold (e.g., 0.2) to ensure the video is not a static-image cheat. First, compare the first and last frames; if the SSIM (Structural Similarity) is above 0.99, reject the sample for lacking dynamics. We only accept samples where the 'Subject Traversal' or 'Environment Change' is visually substantiative and earns its dynamic rating.
  - Mandate 'Identity-Consistency Determinacy' where the subject identified at T=0 is 100% visually identical to the subject at T-final. First, perform a trait-count (Shirt: Blue, Hair: Curly, Object: Jar); if any trait changes without a causal event (like entering a shadow), the sample is rejected. This ensures the data targets 'Persistence' as a core generative quality requirement.
  - Verify 'Background Geometrical Invariance' throughout the camera pan or subject movement. You must evaluate the background for any 'Swimming' or 'Warping' artifacts where pixels appear to crawl independently of the camera motion. An accepted sample shows a rock-solid background that follows the laws of perspective and global motion-consistency.
  - Enforce 'Motion-Naturalness Alignment' where the speed and trajectory of a subject follow standard gravity and momentum. First, check if a falling object or a running animal accelerates and moves in a way that respects the viewer's common-sense physics. Accept the sample if the 'Naturalness' rating (Nat) is based on fluid, high-frame-rate interaction rather than jittery or surreal motion patterns.
* **Rejection Criteria**:
  - Immediately reject 'Static-Image Loops/Cheats' where a single frame is repeated with added digital noise to mimic video. First, perform a motion-vector audit; if all vectors are zero or random noise, the sample is a 'Static result' and must be purged. We aggressively filter for 'Dynamism' to prevent the model from learning that a static image is a valid response to an 'action' prompt.
  - Discard any trajectory with 'Extraneous Entity Hallucinations' where objects or hands suddenly appear out of thin air to fulfill an action. For example, if a prompt asks for a 'person pouring' and a second hand suddenly sprouts from the person's torso to hold the pitcher, reject the sample. This protects the agent from accepting 'Corrupted Results' that violate anatomical or physical norms.
  - Eliminate 'Disjointed Semantic Alignment' where the video is high-quality but completely ignores the image-prompt relationship. First, check if the explosion in the video matches the 'Explosion Type' in the prompt/image (e.g., powder vs. water). If the model generates a generic fire effect when the user provided a water-explosion seed, the sample fails the 'Controllability' (DC) metric and is rejected.
  - Reject trajectories showing 'Severe Motion-Vector Fragmentation' where high-speed areas break into square compression blocks or blurred pixel-soup. First, audit the focal point of the motion; if the subject (e.g., a running horse) becomes unrecognizable due to 'Motion Stutter' or 'Blur Over-saturation,' the sample is rejected for failing the Motion Smoothness (MS) benchmark.
