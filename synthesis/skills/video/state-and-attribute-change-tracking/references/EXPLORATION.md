# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Implement 'Dynamic-Range Amplitude Scanning' to quantify the literal amount of motion by tracking the average pixel-velocity vectors across the entire video. First, you must calculate a 'Motion Heatmap' that highlights areas of high kinetic change versus static background zones. For example, in a video of a bus driving, you would log whether the bus moves 10 pixels or 100 pixels per second to establish its dynamic score.
  - Execute 'Subject/Background Consistency Auditing' by specifically scanning for 'Entity Morphing' or 'Background Warping' during transitions. You should track the unique visual attributes of the main character (e.g., shirt color, hair style) and the background (e.g., building geometry) every 5 frames. A failure case involves a building's window layout shifting or a character's hand gaining an extra finger mid-motion.
  - Conduct 'Motion-Smoothness Frequency Analysis' by inspecting the temporal flow for 'Jitter' or 'Stuttering' artifacts. You must analyze the frame-to-frame delta to ensure that the transition between frames reflects a continuous physical path rather than disjointed 'teleportation' jumps. For instance, if a person is walking, each step should occupy a logically adjacent spatial position without flickering out of existence.
  - Perform 'Semantic-Attribute Fidelity Syncing' by verifying that the dynamic effect (like an explosion or rain) precisely matches the texture of the prompt's category. First, identify the 'Medium' of the effect (e.g., water, fire, wind) and check for appropriate fluid/kinetic behaviors like gravity, transparency, or luminescence. An edge case is a 'fire' effect that lacks luminescence and looks like a static orange sticker, which must be flagged as a fidelity violation.
  - Initiate 'Naturalness Bound Auditing' to judge if the overall physics of the scene—such as reflections, shadows, and gravity—remain coherent. You should check if the subject's shadow moves in sync with the subject and if reflections on a car surface warp illogically during motion. For example, if a car drives past a building but the reflection on the car ignores the building's geometry, document this as a 'Naturalness Failure'.
  - Deploy 'Static-Content Trap Detection' to identify videos that 'cheat' the dynamic requirement by merely duplicating the first frame or using extreme slow motion. You must measure the 'Pixel-Delta' between the start and end of the clip; if the difference is below a 1% threshold despite a high-action prompt, log it as a 'Static Corruption' error. Identifying a video as 'Static-with-Noise' prevents the agent from accepting a frozen frame as a valid dynamic sequence.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - A high-quality trajectory must satisfy an 'Identity-Persistence Threshold' where the subject's key traits (color, shape, identity) survive high-motion vectors without morphing. First, verify that if an actor performs a 360-degree spin, they emerge with the same clothing and facial features they started with. A path that captures a character changing their shirt color mid-spin must be flagged as having failed the consistency audit.
  - The explored path must provide 'Background-Stability Grounding' confirming that the environment does not 'melt' or 'warp' around a moving object. You must ensure that static landmarks (like trees or streetlights) remain stationary and geometrically constant as a vehicle moves past them. If the sidewalk appears to stretch or follow the car, the trajectory captures a 'Generative Hallucination' that is critical for fidelity testing.
  - Ensure the trajectory contains 'Motion-Smoothness Evidence' by documenting at least 3 to 5 'Intermediate Kinetic Keys' that prove a fluid path of travel. First, record the exact position of a moving subject at regular intervals (e.g., T=1s, T=2s, T=3s) to confirm the motion follows a physically plausible trajectory. A 'Smooth' path will show small, consistent changes, whereas a 'Choppy' path will show erratic spatial jumps.
  - The trajectory must accommodate 'Semantic Alignment Proofs' where the visual details specifically fulfill the 'Nuanced Instructions' of a prompt. First, if a prompt asks for a 'playful smile,' ensure the trajectory notes specifically capture the micro-movements of the mouth and eyes, rather than just a static face. A valid path demonstrates that the motion itself is the carrier of the semantic meaning (e.g., the act of 'smiling' is the event, not the facial state).
