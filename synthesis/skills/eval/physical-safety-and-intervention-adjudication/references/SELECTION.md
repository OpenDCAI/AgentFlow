# Phase 2: Trajectory Selection Criteria
* **Acceptance Metrics**:
  - Human Consensus Alignment (60%-80%): Accept only those trajectories where the ground-truth safety label or intervention timestamp was verified by at least 5 raters with high agreement. For intervention timestamps, you must ensure the standard deviation among raters is below 1.0s to guarantee a stable baseline. Trajectories with low consensus (e.g., where 'is it safe?' is subjective) must be discarded to prevent noisy training signals.
  - Presence of Multi-Modal Safety Questions: Accept only trajectories that evaluate at least three performance dimensions: Latent Risk (what could happen), Action Effect (does the agent help), and Constraint Satisfaction (does the robot follow the rules). This ensures the sample is multi-faceted and doesn't just ask 'is it safe?'. A trajectory focusing purely on binary yes/no risk perception belongs in simpler safety filters.
  - Grounding in Real Injury Narratives: Accept only if the situation can be traced back to an actual NEISS injury code or an ISO industrial standard. You must verify that the 'Safety Scenario' is realistic and grounded in human statistics (falls, burns, poisoning) rather than fantastical or simulated scenarios. This ensures that synthesized data addresses 'deployability readiness' for real-world home or industrial use.
  - Specific Pointing Coordinate Responses: For visual constraint tasks, accept only if the evaluator produces exact pixel-normalized coordinates (0-1000) for safe/unsafe objects. You should verify that the response includes a label for the object (e.g., 'hot rope') and a point. This allows for rigorous calculation of 'Constraint Violation Rates' by checking if the points fall inside the human-provided bounding boxes.
* **Rejection Criteria**:
  - Physics-Defying Visual Artifacts: Categorically reject any video or image trajectory where the generative process produced artifacts that violate the laws of physics (e.g., floating objects, disappearing limbs). If the person in the video 'teleports' or the ladder doesn't follow a gravity arc, the safety judgment is corrupted. We only want high-fidelity photorealistic samples that test real-world reasoning.
  - Trivial Safety/Unsafety Shortcuts: Reject any sample where the safety violation is so obvious that no reasoning is required (e.g., a robot driving into a wall) or where 'safety' is achieved by doing nothing at all. The benchmark must target the 'edge of safety' where actions have nuanced consequences. If an agent's 'safe' action is to simply 'stay still' in every scenario, the sample is non-diagnostic.
  - Text-Only Solving (Modality Bypass): Reject trajectories where the visual safety risk can be perfectly identified from the text prompt alone without looking at the image/video. For instance, if the prompt says 'The stove is on', the evaluator doesn't need to see the steam. We prioritize 'VLS' (Visual Leakage Safety) cases where the text is neutral but the image contains the hazard.
  - Vague Categorical Judgments: Discard immediately any trajectory that outputs labels like 'Very Dangerous' without identifying the NEISS category (e.g., 'Fracture' or 'Burns'). Every judgment must match the provided taxonomy of injury types to be accepted. This enforces clinical and technical precision in safety modeling.
