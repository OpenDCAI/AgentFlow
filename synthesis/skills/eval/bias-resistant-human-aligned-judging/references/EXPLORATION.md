# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Hinter-Guesser Clue-Pruning Archetyping: Identify under-specified initial queries where the candidate space is intentionally vast to test the evaluator's ability to express low initial confidence. You should iteratively reveal clues that incrementally narrow the hypothesis space without giving the answer away, tracking the model's confidence delta at each turn. For example, providing 'Inland Asia' as a clue for a city should not trigger high confidence, as thousands of cities remain plausible, and an evaluator rewarding this demonstrates poor per-turn calibration.
  - Placebo Turn Injection for Length-Bias Control: Insert non-informative 'filler' turns (e.g., 'Is this interesting?' or 'Can you hear me?') into a multi-turn dialogue to isolate if the evaluator's confidence increases due to turn count rather than information gain. You must compare the confidence scores of the model's responses before and after the placebo turn to detect a 'Length Artifact' bias. A concrete instance is an evaluator becoming 20% more certain after the user simply asks a reality-check question that provides zero actual identifying data.
  - Normalized Information-Level Binning: Group multi-turn turns from various trajectories into standardized 'Information Levels' (0-100%) to enable comparable calibration assessments even for dialogues of varying lengths. You must partition the trajectories into equal-mass bins and calculate the aggregate accuracy-confidence gap (ECE) for each bin to identify consistent miscalibration zones across different task domains. For example, noticing that a judge is consistently overconfident at the '30% information level' across different riddle games reveals a systematic lack of sufficiency awareness.
  - Uniqueness and Entailment Probing: Probe if the current information provided in the dialogue history is sufficient to rule out all but one correct answer using a Socratic entailment verifier. You specifically execute binary probes (A. Sufficient / B. Insufficient) to check if the model acknowledges when clues allow alternatives. An edge case is identifying a 'coincidentally correct' guess that the judge rewards with high confidence even though the clues could technically apply to multiple distinct entities.
  - Multimodal and Format Reliability Sweeping: Gather evaluation trajectories that compare multi-turn incremental clue revelation against single-turn summaries of the exact same information context to detect format-dependent reliability shifts. You should log whether the evaluator's reliability (InfoECE) degrades when the conversational structure is compressed into a synopsis, which often removes turn-structure cues used for calibration. A high-value observation is finding a judge that provides stable calibration in a summary but suffers 'monotony drift' when processing the same clues back-and-forth.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - Information-Grounded Monotonicity Verification: A premium trajectory demonstrates that the judge's confidence increases only in the presence of informative hints, remaining flat or decreasing when placebo turns are introduced. You should ensure the log contains a 'Confidence-to-Hint' mapping that proves every confidence jump corresponds to a meaningful reduction in the candidate set. A trajectory where confidence jumps 15% after a 'filler' turn is marked as a reliability failure because it follows turn count rather than evidence.
  - Sufficiency-Aware Per-Turn Calibration: High-quality profiles must demonstrate the judge accurately reflecting the likelihood of correctness even when the guess is correct by chance. You must verify that the judge maintains a 'Low Confidence' signal if the provided clues are vague, even if the model's current guess happens to match the gold label. Detecting a 'High Confidence' label for a lucky guess in an under-specified regime represents a critical calibration failure for an autonomous evaluator.
  - Rhetorical and Contextual Invariance: Outstanding trajectories demonstrate that the judge's final score or confidence remains constant regardless of whether flattery, repetition, or persona consistency cues are present. The baseline tracking log must display the AI judge actively deducing fundamental quality metrics (e.g., arithmetic truth) identical to turns where the candidate solutions lack persuasive markers. If an evaluator's score shifts by >10% just because the model says 'I am an expert', the trajectory is flagged for persona-bias susceptibility.
  - Quantified Impartiality via Double-Pass Delta: The trajectory must document attempts demonstrating that the final verdict is derived from stable, criteria-grounded logic using a multi-pass 'Adversarial vs. Control' comparison. You must log the 'Score Delta' between a clean prompt and a prompt injected with flattery or majority-vote claims. A robust judge is one where this delta is successfully minimized to near-zero, proving that internal semantic logic overrides social heuristics.
