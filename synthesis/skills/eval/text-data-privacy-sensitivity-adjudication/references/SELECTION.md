# Phase 2: Trajectory Selection Criteria
* **Acceptance Metrics**:
  - Explicit Identification of Direct vs Indirect PII. Accept only if the trajectory identifies and separates direct identifiers (like 'Darron' or '281-486-7026') from indirect identifiers (like 'working for Florida Dept of Corrections'). This separation is critical for accurate scoring on the Identifiability axis. If the evaluator lists PII as a single amorphous block without categorizing the type, the sample must be discarded as low-precision.
  - Discrete Likert Scale Consistency. Accept only if the evaluation score corresponds strictly to an integer or a rounded float on the provided 1-5 scale with clearly matching descriptions. Trajectories that output labels like 'High' or 'Medium' without matching the numeric rubric nodes fail the structural requirement for this capability. For instance, a score of 4 must be accompanied by the rationale that the text 'clearly contains several identifiers'.
  - Domain-Specific Sensitivity Recognition. Accept sequences where the evaluator accurately recognizes high-risk domains (Mental Health, Legal) as inherently more sensitive even in the absence of names. A valid signal is a rationale stating 'Although no name is present, the specific legal advice request regarding HIV exposure carries extreme risk'. Samples that rate medical/legal distress as 'Harmless' simply because names are omitted demonstrate a failure in sensitivity reasoning.
  - Verification of Global Human-Alignment. Accept only if the exploratory judge's rating falls within a ±1.0 range of the 'Global Human Average' for that specific dataset category. This ensures the synthetic data targets the 'Average Privacy Perspective' found in research papers. If an LLM rates a taco review as 'Extremely Private' (5), while the human baseline is 1.5, the sample is a hallucination of privacy risk and must be rejected.
* **Rejection Criteria**:
  - Identifiability Blindness in Sensitive Contexts. Categorically reject any sample where the judge ignores 'Indirect Identifiers' (e.g., specific dates, unique hospital names) that could lead to doxing. If the evaluator calls a detailed clinical case 'Harmless' just because the patient's name is redacted, it fails to understand modern privacy risks. This protects against training evaluators that are susceptible to simple de-identification bypasses.
  - Publicity Bias Hallucination. Reject any sample where the judge rates a text as 'Harmless' solely because it was 'posted on a public forum like Reddit'. The evaluation must prioritize the *content* over the *location*. If the rationale says 'this is a public review, so it's not private' while ignoring that the review includes the author's home phone number, the judgment is fundamentally flawed.
  - Vague 'Subjective Vibes' Reasoning. Discard immediately any trajectory where the rationale for a rating is purely qualitative and lacks textual evidence (e.g., 'it just feels private'). Every score must be grounded in an extraction of specific segments like names, locations, or sensitive clinical symptoms. Without these textual markers, the judgment cannot be audited and provides zero diagnostic value for synthetic data.
  - Inconsistent Identifiability Thresholding. Reject trajectories where the evaluator applies different sensitivity standards to the same identifiers across different runs. For example, if '281-486-7026' results in a score of 5 in one case but a 2 in another similar case, the judge is unstable. Rejection ensures the resulting dataset represents a coherent, replicable logic for privacy adjudication.
