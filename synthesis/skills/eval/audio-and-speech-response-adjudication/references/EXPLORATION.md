# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Multi-Task Audio-Text Triplet Harvesting: The goal is to discover diverse audio seeds that pair a single auditory source with multiple task instructions such as 'transcribe this,' 'translate this to Thai,' and 'summarize the sentiment.' You must systematically gather clips from public datasets like SeaBench-Audio or YODAS and generate varied candidate responses that range from technically perfect to semantically flawed (hallucinating success). For example, harvesting a YouTube clip of a cooking tutorial and creating a 'wrong' summary that misses a key ingredient mentioned only in the background audio establishes a high-difficulty ground truth.
  - Paralinguistic and Lexical Conflict Seeding: The objective is to obtain 'Hard Negative' evaluation samples where the lexical meaning of the speech directly contradicts the speaker's tone, accent, or emotional paralinguistic cues. You should generate audio where a speaker says something positive in an extremely bored or sarcastic voice, specifically to test if the judge is 'cheating' by only reading the text transcript. An edge case involves finding human-voiced recordings of medical patients who downplay their symptoms while sounds of physical distress are audible in their breathing patterns.
  - Cross-Lingual Honorific and Polysemy Mapping: This strategy seeks to collect audio text in Southeast Asian languages (Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian) where specific words have multiple meanings based on social context or pitch. You must log the specific 'honorific level' of the source audio (e.g., addressing royalty vs. addressing a child) and verify if the candidate translations maintain this hierarchy. A high-value seed is a Thai sentence where the same word is pronounced with a different tone, resulting in a completely different English translation requirement.
  - Non-Speech Event Sequence and Duration Tagging: The goal is to isolate audio clips containing only environmental or human-activity sounds (non-speech) with clear temporal boundaries. You must identify events like 'door slamming,' 'rain starting,' or 'footsteps approaching' and log their exact start and end timestamps. This allows the evaluation agent to synthesize questions that measure an LALM's ability to track 'long-term auditory dependencies' and sequential logic (e.g., 'did the glass break before or after the scream?').
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - Audio-Dependency Integrity Verification: A premium trajectory must demonstrate that the evaluator's final score definitively changed only after processing the audio file, proving it did not rely solely on the text prompt. You must ensure the log records the judge acknowledging that 'the text prompt looks safe, but the audio contains a hidden medical emergency' or 'the text is neutral, but the audio is angry.' This 'Acoustic Grounding' bridge is the primary metric for ensuring the synthetic data tests multimodal reasoning rather than shallow text-matching.
  - Task-Specific Rubric Application Audit: High-quality trajectories show the evaluator utilizing different scoring criteria for different audio tasks (e.g., using Word Error Rate for ASR but Tone-Fidelity for S2TT). You should confirm that the evaluation rationale includes specific references to the 'Rubric'—for instance, stating 'Deducting 1 point for failing to translate the informal Indonesian slang as per the S2TT guideline.' This structural rigor prevents the judge from providing a generic 'Average' score for complex, specialized translations.
  - Paralinguistic-to-Verdict Causal Mapping: Outstanding trajectories include diagnostic notes that explicitly link a paralinguistic feature (micro-pause, pitch rise, emotional tremor) to the final categorical verdict. You must verify that the judge's CoT includes phrases like 'I am labeling this as Machine-generated because the transition between syllables lacks a natural breath intake' or 'Judgment: Sarcastic based on the exaggerated pitch variance.' This granular audit trail ensures the synthesized data accurately measures 'Turing-level' acoustic discrimination.
  - State Change and Event Hierarchy Accuracy: The target profile mandates that for AQA (Audio Question Answering) tasks, the evaluator must verify the 'state transition' described in the response against the raw soundscape. You should confirm that the judge identifies exactly which auditory event was missed—for example, 'The response failed to note that the bird stopped singing when the rain started.' Mapping these hierarchical event dependencies ensures the evaluation captures the agent's ability to model the 'auditory world' rather than just identifying isolated sounds.
