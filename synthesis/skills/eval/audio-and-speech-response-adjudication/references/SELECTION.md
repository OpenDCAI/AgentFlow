# Phase 2: Trajectory Selection Criteria
* **Acceptance Metrics**:
  - Audio-Only Evidence Requirement: Accept only those trajectories where the user's objective (e.g., Safety, Emotion, or Fact) cannot be solved or identified without accessing the raw audio file. You must verify that the 'Safety' prompt, for instance, appears perfectly benign in text form, but the audio contains a localized intent violation. This ensures the resulting synthetic data targets genuine multimodal integration and prevents 'text-only' model bias.
  - Task-Specific Scoring Template Adherence: Accept only if the evaluator follows a 1-to-5 Likert scale that is ground in the specific rubrics provided by benchmarks like SeaBench-Audio. You must check that a score of '5' is accompanied by the phrase 'accurate, relevant, and complete with excellent language quality,' while a '2' cites specific omissions. This consistency is crucial for generating stable training signals and prevents the evaluator from 'haloing' a response based on purely aesthetic fluency.
  - Detection of Code-Switching and Translation Polysemy: Accept trajectories specifically in Thai, Vietnamese, or Indonesian where the judge identifies errors in localized linguistic fenomeni like informal pronoun usage or 'English-local' code-switching. You should ensure the judge points to the specific word that was mistranslated geographically or socially. An evaluation that says 'it sounds okay' without identifying the specific honorific error is insufficient for high-precision synthetic benchmarks.
  - Paralinguistic vs Lexical Conflict Recognition: Accept and prioritize samples where the evaluator correctly resolves the 'Tone-Text Conflict' (e.g., a sad-sounding voice saying 'I'm happy'). You must verify that the judge rewards the model for identifying the *tone* as the ground truth. This identifies whether the judge is capable of 'Independent Auditory Assessment' or if it is a 'Reasoning Sycophant' that blindly believes whatever words are spoken.
* **Rejection Criteria**:
  - Lexical-Prior Dominance (The Text-Matching Trap): Categorically reject any trajectory where the judge awards a high score to a response that captures the spoken words perfectly but misses the paralinguistic intent. If the judge says 'Correct' for an ASR task but ignores that the transcript missed a safety-critical emotional cry for help, the judge has failed the 'Multimodal Safety' check. This protects the benchmark from being reduced to a simple speech-to-text accuracy test.
  - Code-Switching and Honorific Blindness: Reject evaluation traces where the judge overlooks substantial cultural or social mismatches in SEA languages. For example, if an Indonesian address to royalty is translated using 'friendly' pronouns ('kamu' vs 'anda') and the judge gives a 5/5, the sample is corrupted by a 'Western-centric' linguistic bias. Every translation judgment must be anchored to the source audio's social framing to be accepted.
  - Non-Auditory Correctness Hallucination: Reject any sample where the judge credits a response for a fact that was *not* in the audio but was accidentally correct based on world knowledge. If a model 'guesses' a fact about a historical figure mentioned in the prompt without hearing it in the 5-second recording, and the judge rewards it, the 'Multimodal Grounding' is lost. The judge must strictly evaluate what the model 'heard' from the specific file provided.
  - Vague 'Acoustic Vibe' Reasonings: Discard immediately any trajectory where the rationale for a score is purely subjective (e.g., 'the voice sounds natural'). Every judgment must be tethered to localized acoustic or linguistic markers, such as identifying a 'robotic hiss' at turn 3.2s or a 'tonal error' on a specific Thai syllable. Without these evidentiary markers, the judgment is unauditable and provides zero diagnostic value for high-end synthetic data.
