# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Construct a 'Domain Entity & Abbreviation Ledger' before translation. The agent must scan the document for specific identifiers, such as law codes (BV, PatG, LBI), technical standards, or regulatory bodies, and explicitly map them to their target-language counterparts using an internal cross-reference list. This step ensures that critical entity markers remain factually accurate and legally binding in the new language. For example, if a Swiss document mentions 'LBI' in French, the agent must log the transition to 'PatG' in German before drafting a single word.
  - Implement a 'Pseudo-Document Chunking' roadmap for long articles. For documents exceeding 500 tokens, the agent should divide the text into chunks of approximately 10 sentences to maintain inter-sentential context while staying within the model's high-precision window. This prevents the 'forgotten context' failure where the start of the document and the end use different terms for the same subject. A concrete step involves the agent recording the final state of Chunk N to ensure Chunk N+1 begins with the same narrative tone and entity names.
  - Execute a 'Script-Specific Perceptual Scan' for non-Latin or diacritic-heavy targets. The agent must identify whether the target language requires Ge'ez, Arabic, or tone-marked Latin scripts and adjust its internal validation hooks to detect character-level corruption early. This ensures that the generated tokens are not just phonetically similar but are orthographically correct according to the specific region's standards. An edge case is Yorùbá, where the agent must explicitly search for words that change meaning based on sub-dots or accents to avoid 'flat' text errors.
  - Audit 'Interpretive Nuance' zones within long-form summaries. In interpretive texts like legal headnotes or medical reports, the agent must identify abstract concepts (e.g., 'principles of industrial economy') that require functional equivalents rather than literal translations. The agent should note these phrases in the ledger and search for target-language professional standards that match the intent. An edge case is a term like 'Bankgeheimnis', which might be literally 'bank secret' but should be professionally rendered as 'Bank client confidentiality' in a formal US/UK legal context.
  - Perform a 'Cross-Sentence Cohesion Audit' to link dropped subjects. In many languages like Swahili or Yorùbá, the agent must look back at the overarching document context to resolve pronouns or implied subjects that may have been omitted in the source sentence. This prevents 'Reference Drifting' where the translation accidentally attributes an action to the wrong person because it lost the thread of the previous paragraph. A concrete step is logging the 'Active Subject' in the exploration ledger and updating it only when a new thematic actor is introduced.
* **Target Trajectory Profile**:
  - The trajectory demonstrates 'Regulated Script Mapping' for non-standard alphabets. The agent should show how it validated the target script's integrity (e.g., checking Ge'ez character rendering) before proceeding to the final output. If the agent merely outputs text without documenting a check for script-specific characters or tone markers, the trajectory is deemed too generic for professional translation. An example is logging the selection of specific Amharic glyphs over simplified alternatives to maintain formal legal formatting.
  - The trajectory shows visible 'Register Calibration' based on the document type. The path should reflect a decision-making process where the agent chooses a formal, professional, or technical tone (e.g., selecting 'provided' over 'given') that matches the source document's authority. This is especially important for legal and press release translations where tone conveys legitimacy. A concrete example is logging: 'Source is an official Supreme Court decision; adopting formal legal register for French output to maintain authority'.
  - The trajectory documents 'Resolution of Cross-Sentence Ambiguity' to ensure cohesion. For documents with dropped subjects or ambiguous pronouns, the trajectory must show the agent looking back to earlier sentences to confirm who is acting or which law is being cited. This proves that the translation is discourse-aware rather than a series of independent sentence-level guesses. A strong trace logs: 'Resolving pronoun "il" in Paragraph 2 by referencing "Le Tribunal fédéral" identified in Paragraph 1'.
  - The trajectory demonstrates a 'Chunk-Alignment Balance' check to prevent under-generation. The path must show the agent comparing the source sentence count against the translated sentence count for every pseudo-document chunk to ensure no content was swallowed by the model. A high-quality trace will explicitly record: 'Source chunk has 10 sentences; Translation chunk has 10 sentences; alignment verified.' An edge case is identifying a model's tendency to merge two sentences into one and forcing a re-split to maintain the original document's structure.
