# Phase 2: Trajectory Selection Criteria
* **Acceptance Metrics**:
  - Chain or map completeness. Accept the sample strictly if the full landmark chain or global-to-local path can be unequivocally reconstructed from the stored trajectory frames. The selector must manually verify that each relational hop or map-waypoint is visually apparent and causally linked to the final answer. An example passes flawlessly when the anchoring building, the eastward travel, and the specific target shop are executed in strict sequence.
  - Cross-scale navigation necessity. The trajectory must objectively demonstrate both long-range regional transit and localized short-range verification to be considered valid. Evaluators should unhesitatingly reject paths that remain exclusively at a high altitude or exclusively at a ground-level crawl throughout the clip. The benchmark strictly targets the intellectual transition between global situational awareness and local embodied focus.
  - Semantic payload grounding certainty. Accept only if the final semantic answer (e.g., color, count, shop type) is irrefutably tied to the correct map coordinate or landmark sequence rather than a lucky background distractor. The payload answer must be confirmed purely by the direct final observation, eliminating any possibility of the model guessing based on world priors. This ensures the trajectory physically provided the necessary and sufficient information.
  - Traceable ambiguity reduction. A high-quality selected trajectory should exhibit a clear trace of how semantic ambiguity dissolved over time as landmarks were sequentially confirmed. The selector should be able to pinpoint the exact frame where incorrect regions or distractor vehicles were decisively ruled out by the agent’s movement. Trajectories that jump straight to the answer without a traceable narrowing phase must be filtered out.
  - Urban-scale challenge maintenance. The sample must display a serious open-space challenge featuring realistic object similarity while remaining cleanly solvable via disciplined exploration. The selector should preserve environments with reasonable distractors (like multiple similar shops) but reject scenes choked with impenetrable visual noise or millions of identical instances. The goal is challenging structural navigation, not finding a microscopic needle in an infinite haystack.
* **Rejection Criteria**:
  - Absence of genuine macro-navigation. Summarily reject any sample where the final target could be localized and verified via direct local inspection from the starting frame. If the prompt grandiosely mentions a massive building but the target car is already a central, obvious feature in frame one, the landmark framing is purely decorative. Such samples falsely inflate the model's apparent capability and corrupt the training signal.
  - Static scale failure. Reject any instance where the trajectory fatally fails to bridge the gap between coarse, high-level map localization and detailed local answer extraction. A sweeping aerial city clip that simply halts in the sky without zooming into the targeted street-level shop frontage offers insufficient localized data. Similarly, starting deep inside the target zone with no approach phase invalidates the sample.
  - Spatial mismatch or hallucination. Reject questions whose requested map coordinates, named landmarks, or relational clauses do not physically align with the actual simulated evidence. The selector must cross-reference every spatial instruction against the recorded frames and swiftly discard items with false or physically unverifiable geometry. If an instruction claims a shop is north but it is undeniably south, it cannot be salvaged.
  - Excessive open-vocabulary guessing. Reject data if the required semantic payload, even after perfect spatial grounding, demands unsupported external world inference rather than raw observable evidence. For example, deducing the exact corporate owner of a featureless, unmarked warehouse based solely on its roof shape crosses into hallucination testing. The final answer must always be derivable from the visual textures and objects physically within the local view.
  - Simulation-perception disconnect. Reject trajectories where the agent supposedly navigates via a hierarchical plan but executes a series of waypoints that physically clip through buildings or ignore obvious terrain obstacles. The path connecting the macro-waypoints must remain grounded in realistic control physics. Magically teleporting through a skyscraper to reach the correct coordinate nullifies the embodied navigation constraint.
