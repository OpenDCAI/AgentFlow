# Phase 2: Trajectory Selection Criteria
* **Acceptance Metrics**:
  - 100% Signal-to-Text Fidelity ensuring no 'Ghost Content' from background noise is present in the final answer. Accept the trajectory only if every word in the translation can be traced back to the 'Primary Speaker' rather than 'Babble Noise' chatter. For instance, if an airport announcement is translated but the speaker was asking for a sandwich, the sample must be rejected.
  - Isomorphism of Propositional Units between the audio and the translation. The sample is authorized if the number of 'actions' or 'factions' heard in the speech (after disfluency removal) matches the number in the final text. A valid sample ensures that if the speaker eventually stated three instructions despite stuttering, the final text lists precisely those three instructions.
  - Zero Target Character-Error Rate (CER) degradation under ambient noise. The selection filter checks if the output avoids the 'stuttering text' or 'broken tokens' that typically occur when a model's decoder is confused by static. Accept only clean, grammatical sentences that show zero evidence of the mechanical acoustic corruption.
  - Affective Aura Alignment where the 'Vibe' of the translation matches the 'Prosody' of the voice. Accept the sample if the translation sounds emotionally appropriate (e.g., angry, polite, or distressed) based on the audio evidence. For instance, if the audio is a high-speed complaint, the Indonesian text must sound frustrated and urgent rather than a flat robot-like report.
* **Rejection Criteria**:
  - Source-Language Leakage or Off-Target Residue in the target string. Reject any trajectory where segments of the audio remain untranslated or where the model 'gives up' and outputs English tokens in a French translation. This typically happens when speech is too fast; any sample with mixed-language output (unless requested) is a failure.
  - Insertion Hallucination triggered by babble or static. Exclude any output where the agent 'invents' sentences to fill noisy gaps in the audio recording. A clear rejection is a case where five seconds of static is translated into a coherent but entirely fictional noun phrase or sentence.
  - Literal Stutter Mirroring where disfluencies are translated as text repetitions. Discard samples where the agent 'fixes' nothing and instead translates 'I... I... go' as 'Je... Je... vais.' This represents a failure to understand the disfluency-resolution capability, which requires generating fluent target-language content.
  - Under-Generation via Lazy Gist Summarization. Reject any trajectory where a detailed 30-second audio clip containing specific names and dates is reduced to a one-sentence summary like 'The man is talking about his flight.' The translation must remain source-aligned and preserve all recoverable details.
