# Phase 1: Environment Exploration Guide
* **Exploration Strategy**:
  - Identify Generic Human Nouns within the source text pool to target socio-linguistic bias. You must scan for plural role nouns like 'scientists' or 'citizens' and determine if they refer to a generic group or specific known individuals. For example, 'The scientists at NASA' is a generic plural that triggers fair translation, whereas 'The scientists John and Mary' requires specific gender accuracy instead.
  - Map Italian Morphological Inflections that facilitate the generic masculine default. You need to identify every noun and article in the target Italian that would usually end in '-i' or '-o' to create a list of 'bias-prone' nodes for transformation. An edge case involves identifying already neutral words like 'docente' where only the surrounding article needs inclusive adjustment.
  - Assign a specific Inclusivity Strategy based on the user's register or requirements. You will evaluate whether to apply Neutralization (e.g., using 'personale'), Visibility (e.g., dual forms), or Neomorphism (e.g., schwa or asterisk). For instance, in a social media context, a neomorpheme like 'ricercatorə' may be selected, while a formal report would favor a collective noun.
  - Audit Document-Level Gender Consistency to ensure the strategy is applied across all sentences. You must scan the full context to verify that a role neutralized in sentence one doesn't accidentally revert to a masculine form in sentence four. A failure to avoid is a mixed-strategy output where some paragraphs are inclusive and others are biased.
  - Verify Contextual Specificity to prevent the neutralization of named people. You must strictly check if the subject is an actual historical figure or a named entity before applying fairness rules, as forcing neutrality on a specific woman like 'Marie Curie' is a factual error. For example, if translating a biography of specific women, you must use feminine forms, not neutral collectives.
