# Phase 2: Trajectory Selection Criteria
* **Acceptance Metrics**:
  - Verify a 'Monotonicity Rank Correlation' (τ) of at least 0.60 for the target answer throughout the multi-turn session. Accept a trajectory only if the confidence scores strictly track the arrival of task-relevant evidence. Use a pairwise comparison of every turn (i < j) and confirm that cd,j >= cd,i in a significant majority of pairs. A representative rejection is an agent that becomes less confident after a clue like 'The city is in Japan' if the previous clues already established 'Asia'.
  - Demand 'InfoECE Delta Compliance' by verifying that the mean absolute error between accuracy and confidence is bounded within a 15% range across all normalized information levels. The selection phase must verify that the agent's 'Sufficiency Probes' are correctly aligned with the actual empirical accuracy of the model. If a model is consistently over-confident in the early turns (0-20% info), the sample is rejected. This prevents the generation of sycophantic 'Yes-man' confidence signals.
  - Confirm 'Placebo Independence' ensuring the Delta-Confidence for non-informative turns is at least 3x smaller than for informative turns. The selector must audit the 'Reasoning Trace' to confirm the agent identified the placebo turn as 'conversational filler' rather than 'task evidence.' A trajectory where a placebo turns triggers a confidence jump equal to a real hint is a hard failure. This ensures the model learns to ignore 'chatter' when assessing task-identifiability.
  - Assess 'Sufficiency vs. Correctness Distinction' by checking the P(SUFFICIENT) score specifically. The trajectory is accepted only if the confidence score (P-Sufficient) remains low (e.g., < 0.5) until the moment the hints uniquely identify the goal, regardless of whether the agent's guess was lucky. This metric ensures the synthetic data supports high-stakes decision-making where 'guessing right' is not the same as 'knowing why.'
  - Confirm 'Multi-turn vs. Single-turn Accuracy Neutrality' ensuring the agent's task accuracy is not significantly degraded by the interactive format. Accept trajectories where the multi-turn accuracy (zd,i) is comparable to a single-turn summary (Sd,i). This ensures the data targets 'Confidence Dynamics' rather than overcoming the 'Lost in the Middle' error. A successful selection ensures the agent is performing at its cognitive limit while accurately reporting its doubt.
* **Rejection Criteria**:
  - Reject 'Turn-Index Hallucinations' where the model's confidence increases linearly with the number of turns regardless of information content. This represents a shallow length-bias that destroys the utility of the confidence signal for real-world agents. If a model shows τ > 0.8 for both informative and placebo-heavy trajectories, it indicates a failure to differentiate information from length and must be purged.
  - Discard 'Confidence-Accuracy Decoupling' where the model provides a high confidence score (over 0.9) for an answer that is factually incorrect according to the gold label. While some uncertainty is expected, 'High-Certainty Hallucinations' are the primary target for mitigation. We specifically reject samples that provide a 'false sense of security' to the user, as these are the most dangerous outputs for a deployed assistant.
  - Eliminate 'Surface-Level Consistently' where the confidence is high solely because the model sampled the same wrong answer 10 times (Self-Consistency failure). The selection phase must verify that 'Confidence' is anchored in 'Sufficiency' (evidence-based) rather than just 'Agreement' (repetition-based). If the agent correctly guesses an entity but cannot derive it from the clues, the P(SUFFICIENT) must be low to be accepted.
  - Refuse 'Robotic Calibration' where the agent always outputs a fixed confidence score (e.g., 50%) throughout the interaction. The agent must show 'Dynamic Sensitivity'—the score must move in response to the clues. Any trace where the confidence variance across 10 turns is near zero is rejected as a failure of adaptive reasoning. This ensures the data teaches the model to 'be more certain' only when it has 'learned more'.
  - Reject 'Format-Induced Drift' where the agent's confidence in a single-turn summary is 30% higher than in the equivalent multi-turn interaction without an accuracy increase. This indicates that the model is 'overwhelmed' or 'misled' by the compressed format. The selection process ensures that the 'Interactive Structure' of the dialogue is respected and that the confidence signal is robust across both presentation styles.
